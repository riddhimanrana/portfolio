On March 27, at 9:42 p.m., a student emailed me asking if I was going to apply for a CSF officer position. The application was due that night. I had about two hours.

I replied three minutes later. First I thanked her for actually reaching out. Then I asked which position would let me work with organizations and bring Let's Assist into CSF. She suggested webmaster or secretary.

That exchange belongs in this story because I could have missed it entirely. By then, I'd spent months trying to get clubs and organizations to try the platform. A student taking a few minutes to send me an application link opened a different route: work with the people running the club and build the tools alongside them.

Today, Let's Assist has 900+ users, and DVHS CSF is using it for activity posts, attendance, and point submissions. The path here was a lot less clean than that sentence makes it sound.

## Before CSF

I wrote about the [early versions of Let's Assist](/blog/building-lets-assist) before. The problem was something I'd run into myself: finding a volunteer event, signing up somewhere, recording hours somewhere else, and eventually getting someone to verify them. I wanted that whole process in one place.

Once I'd built it, I started reaching out. By January, I'd contacted more than 70 clubs, organizations, teachers, administrators, PTA members, and district staff for feedback and interest. I'd find an event they were already running and make a mock-up of it on Let's Assist so they could see their own work in the product. I kept a tracker in Notion with contacts, meeting notes, and follow-ups.

Looking through that tracker now, there's a lot of waiting. A follow-up after winter break. Another email after a meeting. A reminder before the end of the school year. Somewhere in between, homework and competitions still had to happen.

At Windemere Ranch Middle School, Ms. Erickson had tried the platform and given me requests I could build around: recurring events, calendar integration, and saved drafts. In October, she wanted to create a signup for students helping clean her classroom after school. I'd just added the trusted-member approval step, so she got blocked trying to create the project. I approved her account, then had to finish and test the changes I'd promised while also dealing with chapter tests and Google's calendar verification.

That was one of my first reminders that a teacher trying to use the app didn't care where I was in the development process. She needed a signup that worked.

Ms. Gallegos, the WRMS CJSF advisor, raised a different issue: she couldn't officially endorse a platform the district hadn't approved. I was a former member of that CJSF program, so this was exactly the kind of use I'd hoped to support.

I spoke at the SRVUSD Board of Education meeting on October 14, 2025. Afterward, Ms. Erickson suggested working with my CS teacher on the approval process. My AP CS teacher submitted an IT verification request. Later, my counselor referred me to our assistant principal, who contacted district IT as well.

![Speaking at the SRVUSD Board of Education meeting](./lets-assist-beyond-the-demo/board-meeting.png)

*The October 14 board meeting. My section of the [public recording starts at 1:29:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jCHJ60q5PjY&t=5394s).*

Those conversations didn't turn into district approval. Part of what I was trying to resolve was basic access: allowing younger students to sign in with their school Google accounts and receive the platform's emails. In January, I was still asking how to get a direct conversation with IT about privacy, and trying to answer another question that kept coming up: who would keep this running after I graduated?

I understood why people asked. I was asking them to move real events and student records into software built by a high schooler. A good demo couldn't answer every concern about support or what would happen a few years later.

## The meeting where I started building too fast

On January 8, I followed up with Ms. Matsumoto after talking to her at school about DV Interact. She watched the demo and brought in Mr. Gerbert, another Interact advisor and the local Rotary club president. We met during lunch on January 12.

They already used SignUpGenius. They had actual volunteer processes to walk me through, including waivers and event registration. I went back to building. By January 19, I was sending screenshots of event drafts, organization reports, Google Sheets syncing, and electronic waivers.

The conversation moved toward Truck Time, then Run for Education. I started working through what those events would need. I was trying to fit meetings around school dismissal and tests, and sending follow-ups when we couldn't connect.

On January 30, Mr. Gerbert told me the Education Foundation board had decided to use an established system with tech support for that year's events.

I'd already been building for those workflows. That was frustrating. But he still wanted to help me find somewhere to test the product, and I sent a long reply about the credibility problem I kept facing. I didn't want Let's Assist to be something I made for college applications and abandoned after high school. I wanted people to believe I'd keep working on it.

We kept talking about other possibilities, including involving incoming Interact officers. Those conversations mattered even though they didn't become a rollout for Truck Time or Run for Education. They also taught me to check whether an organization was ready to switch before spending another weekend on its custom features.

## Troop 941 gave me somewhere real to start

My troop was different. People there knew me, and the platform had already been used for real events. I could talk to the leaders who organized them and see what happened after someone clicked the signup link.

In February, I presented Let's Assist to Troop 941. Standing next to a projector outdoors is a very different experience from sending somebody a demo video. The people looking at the screen were the people who would have to use it.

![Presenting Let's Assist to Troop 941 outdoors](./lets-assist-beyond-the-demo/troop-presentation.jpg)

*The Troop 941 presentation. You can also read the [slides](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1lVeFxzUxJy5fYManfnKKaGm5ZL4MEiE0SFMuL2PEHp4/edit).*

The troop moved into using it for event coordination, member management, reports, and waivers. Calendar syncing and Eagle project signups gave me concrete problems to solve. I could bring screenshots of actual use to my next school meeting.

There were other small openings too. DV Hearts4Hands agreed in February to try it at an upcoming volunteering event. My notes from that first trial record six signups and a snag getting the link to CSF in time. That's the kind of detail a user count doesn't tell you. The signup page can work, but people still need to hear about it before the event.

Through an Eagle project connection, I also spoke with the Grand Knight of Knights of Columbus Council 6043. Their needs were different again. Some members weren't comfortable making accounts, and the group wanted manual signups and CSV imports. That conversation pushed me to think about volunteers who would never use the platform the way I did.

The local PTA conversations raised questions about liability and who stood behind the software. An SRV Council PTA event gave me another chance to learn from real coordination work. Getting a group interested and getting it ready to commit were often months apart.

## The district process kept going

On February 24, CITE Privacy Services invited Let's Assist to review and sign a California student data privacy agreement. I hadn't signed it. Receiving the invitation was a step in the process, and there was still work to do.

On March 2, I met with SRVUSD's director of technology, Ms. Hilton, and presented the platform with my parents. I followed up with the [presentation slides](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1phDuxMQquoa5XFfrF7rVTY77eWkAuDZk7CLPHuadvXU/edit) and a demo organization her team could try.

In May, she told me she'd shared the presentation with the management team and referred the data privacy discussion to the district's data manager. I offered documentation, privacy policies, and data maps for review. By late May, we were also discussing formal agreements under Tulip Coaching LLC.

There wasn't a signed district agreement at the end of that email chain. I still think the progress is worth documenting. It meant learning what a district needed to review, then following through on the paperwork and questions alongside the software.

## CSF needed its own product

An earlier CSF conversation hadn't gone anywhere. My notes said the club's existing way of sharing opportunities didn't fit what I was proposing. I had treated CSF too much like another organization that needed an event signup page.

Working on its actual operations showed me how much I'd missed.

CSF has membership records and school-year terms. Attendance matters. Students submit evidence for points, and officers have to review it against rules about activity and drive limits. Records already exist in spreadsheets, including records for people who haven't created a Let's Assist account.

Importing those records took a lot of work. Matching two people because their names looked similar would be a terrible shortcut. An account connection needs evidence. Conflicting records need a review path. Officers need to see where imported information came from and decide what to do with it.

The less visible work included recovering interrupted imports, preserving older records, and making sure an attendance entry could exist before the student joined the app. I spent a lot of time on those cases. They're easy to leave out of a demo, but an officer eventually runs into them.

By the September CSF meeting, we were introducing Let's Assist as the place for posts, activities, and point submissions, replacing the old Google Classroom workflow.

![Introducing Let's Assist at the DVHS CSF meeting in the gym](./lets-assist-beyond-the-demo/csf-meeting.jpg)

*The DVHS CSF meeting. A lot of the work behind this screen was getting existing records and officer workflows to fit together.*

There's still work to do. Account connections can need review, imported records can disagree, and every new group brings another way of organizing volunteers. I'm much more careful now about calling something finished just because I can show it on my own screen.

I wanted to write this down while the emails were still easy to find. The student who sent that application link, the troop leaders who tried the platform, and the adults who made time to talk all changed what I built. So did the groups that chose to keep using something else.

For now, I'm still working on [Let's Assist](https://lets-assist.com). The next useful improvement usually comes from someone trying to get their actual work done.
